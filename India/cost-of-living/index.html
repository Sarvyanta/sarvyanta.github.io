Cost of Living in India

Housing, food, transport, utilities and everyday expenses — explained in a practical way for people living in India and people planning to visit or move here.

See Monthly Budgets How We Estimate
INDIA 2026
₹

There is no single cost of living in India.

Your monthly spending can change dramatically depending on the city, neighbourhood, housing choice, family size and lifestyle.

QUICK ANSWER

How much money do you need to live in India?

The answer depends mainly on where you live and how you live. A person renting a room and cooking at home can have a very different monthly budget from a family living in a large metropolitan city with private schooling, a car and frequent restaurant meals.

Instead of giving one number for the whole country, this guide breaks the cost of living into the categories that normally matter most: housing, food, transport, utilities, education, healthcare and lifestyle.

Important: The budget figures in this article are planning estimates, not official government price quotations. Actual expenses vary by city, locality, household and lifestyle.
THE MAIN EXPENSES

Where does the money go?

For most households, a small number of categories account for the majority of everyday spending.

01

Housing

Rent or home-related costs are often the largest difference between living in a smaller city and living in a major metropolitan area.

  • Rent or home loan
  • Maintenance
  • Deposit and moving costs
  • Furniture and household setup
02

Food

Food costs can be relatively flexible because households can choose between home cooking, local markets, restaurants, delivery and different levels of packaged food.

  • Groceries
  • Vegetables and fruits
  • Milk and household essentials
  • Restaurants and food delivery
03

Transport

Transport spending depends heavily on whether you use public transport, two-wheelers, taxis or a private car.

  • Fuel
  • Public transport
  • Ride-hailing
  • Vehicle maintenance
04

Utilities

Electricity, water, cooking fuel, internet and mobile services are recurring household expenses.

  • Electricity
  • Water
  • Internet
  • Mobile services
05

Education

Education can range from low-cost public options to substantial private-school, tuition and activity expenses.

  • School fees
  • Tuition
  • Books and supplies
  • Activities
06

Lifestyle

Entertainment, shopping, travel, subscriptions and dining out can make a large difference to discretionary spending.

  • Shopping
  • Entertainment
  • Dining out
  • Travel and recreation
CITY MATTERS

India does not have one cost of living

The same income can produce very different lifestyles depending on the city and neighbourhood.

LARGE METROS

Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru

Housing can represent a significant share of the household budget. Transport, dining and lifestyle choices can also increase monthly spending.

MAJOR CITIES

Hyderabad, Pune, Chennai

Costs vary substantially by locality. Choosing housing outside premium areas can make a major difference.

SMALLER CITIES & TOWNS

Regional India

Housing and some everyday services can be less expensive, although salaries, availability and lifestyle options also differ from larger cities.

PRACTICAL PLANNING

Example monthly budgets

These are illustrative planning models rather than official cost-of-living figures.

SIMPLE INDIVIDUAL

Essential lifestyle

₹20,000–₹35,000

Suitable as a planning example for a person using modest accommodation, cooking regularly and controlling discretionary spending.

  • Shared/modest accommodation
  • Mostly home-cooked food
  • Public transport or two-wheeler
  • Limited discretionary spending
COMFORTABLE INDIVIDUAL

Balanced lifestyle

₹35,000–₹60,000+

A broader planning range for someone with private accommodation, regular transport and moderate spending on restaurants and entertainment.

  • Private accommodation
  • Regular dining out
  • Two-wheeler or mixed transport
  • Entertainment and shopping
FAMILY

Family household

₹60,000–₹1,50,000+

Family budgets can vary enormously depending on rent, school choices, transport, healthcare and lifestyle.

  • Family housing
  • Groceries and household expenses
  • School and tuition
  • Transport and healthcare
Why is the range so wide?

Because housing and education choices alone can change a household budget by a large amount. A person living with family may also have a completely different cost structure from someone renting independently.

EVERYDAY CHOICES

Ways people can reduce everyday costs

01

Choose housing carefully

Rent can dominate a monthly budget. Compare the total cost of rent, commute and utilities rather than looking only at the advertised rent.

02

Cook more often

Home cooking can give households greater control over food spending, especially when eating out becomes frequent.

03

Compare transport options

A shorter commute, public transport or a two-wheeler may sometimes reduce the total cost of travel.

04

Track recurring expenses

Subscriptions, delivery fees and small recurring purchases can become significant when added together over a year.

How the cost of living is changing

India's household spending patterns have changed alongside urbanisation, technology, incomes, infrastructure and consumer choices.

THEN

Earlier generations

Many households relied more heavily on local markets, cash payments, neighbourhood services and family-based living arrangements.

NOW

Today's India

Digital payments, online shopping, food delivery, ride-hailing, apartments and subscription services have become part of everyday life for many households.

NEXT

What could change?

AI assistants, digital services, smarter homes, changing transport systems and new forms of work could influence household spending further.

Note: The "Next" section describes possibilities, not predictions or guarantees.

SOURCES & METHODOLOGY

How we estimate cost-of-living information

Cost-of-living information changes over time. Sarvyanta distinguishes between official statistics, observed market ranges and illustrative household budgets.

Official statistics

Government statistical publications are used when discussing national price trends and inflation.

Planning estimates

Example budgets are clearly identified as planning ranges rather than official prices.

City differences

Housing, transport and lifestyle costs can differ greatly between cities and even between neighbourhoods.

Regular updates

Numbers that become outdated should be reviewed before being treated as current guidance.

Primary statistical reference

India's Consumer Price Index is published by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation and tracks changes in retail prices across major consumption categories.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is India cheap to live in?

India can offer relatively affordable options, but the answer depends heavily on city, housing, income and lifestyle. Major metropolitan areas can have substantially higher housing costs.

Which expense usually matters most?

Housing is often one of the biggest differences between household budgets. Education, transport and lifestyle choices can also have a major impact.

Is ₹30,000 enough to live in India?

It can be possible for some individuals, particularly with modest accommodation and controlled spending, but it may be difficult in some expensive cities if private accommodation and other major expenses are included.

Is the cost of living the same across India?

No. Costs vary between cities, towns, neighbourhoods and households. Housing is one of the strongest sources of variation.

Are the budget figures official government figures?

No. The example budgets on this page are illustrative planning ranges. Official statistics such as CPI are used separately as references for broader price trends.
