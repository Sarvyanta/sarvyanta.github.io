How Did India Move From There to Here?

From bicycles, buses and railway counters to metros, navigation apps and electric mobility.

THEN NOW NEXT
THE JOURNEY

Transportation is more than vehicles. It is how a country connects.

The way Indians travel has changed because of roads, railways, cities, technology, income, infrastructure and changing lifestyles.

A journey that once required a paper ticket and careful planning can now begin with a smartphone.

THREE ERAS

India's transportation journey

01
THEN

Travel needed planning

Earlier generations often planned journeys around fixed routes, timetables, tickets and information available locally.

  • Walking and bicycles were important for short trips.
  • Buses connected towns and neighbourhoods.
  • Railways were central to long-distance travel.
  • Railway tickets were often purchased at counters.
  • Auto-rickshaws and scooters became important urban transport.
  • Paper maps were useful.
  • Long journeys often required more preparation.
Imagine it: Before leaving home, you needed to know the route, timing and ticket arrangements much more carefully.
→
02
NOW

Travel is connected

Smartphones have changed the travel experience almost as much as the vehicles themselves.

  • Metro systems serve several major urban regions.
  • Ride-hailing apps connect passengers and drivers.
  • Navigation apps provide live route information.
  • Rail, bus and flight tickets can be booked online.
  • FASTag supports electronic toll payments.
  • Electric two-wheelers and three-wheelers are increasingly visible.
  • Digital payments are part of everyday travel.
Current transition: Government EV programmes are supporting electric mobility, charging infrastructure and the wider EV ecosystem in India. 3
→
03
NEXT

Mobility may become intelligent

The future may combine electric vehicles, connected infrastructure, automation and AI-assisted planning.

  • More electric vehicles.
  • Smarter charging infrastructure.
  • AI-assisted route planning.
  • More connected vehicles.
  • Better integration between buses, metro, rail and cabs.
  • Autonomous vehicles may appear in selected environments.
  • Mobility could become increasingly software-driven.
Important: Autonomous transport and other future technologies will depend on safety, regulation, infrastructure, economics and public acceptance.
