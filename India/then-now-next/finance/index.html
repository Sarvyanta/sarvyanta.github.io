How Did Money Change in India?

From cash, passbooks and trusted local relationships to UPI, mobile banking and a future shaped by intelligent technology.

THEN NOW NEXT
THE BIG PICTURE

Money changed. The reasons behind it changed even more.

The way Indians earn, save, pay, borrow and invest has changed dramatically across generations. Technology is one part of the story. Banking access, infrastructure, regulation, consumer behaviour and changing lifestyles also played major roles.

This page looks at that journey in three stages: Then, Now and Next.

THE JOURNEY

Three generations of financial life

01
THEN

Money you could hold

For many families, financial life was strongly connected to physical money, local banks, familiar people and long-term saving.

  • Cash was central to everyday payments.
  • Bank branches and passbooks were important.
  • Cheques were widely used for larger payments.
  • Post Office savings were familiar to many households.
  • Gold was an important form of household wealth.
  • Insurance often involved personal agents.
  • Investment information was harder to access.
Imagine it: A financial transaction could mean a visit, a signature, a paper document and sometimes a long wait.
→
02
NOW

Money in your phone

Today, a smartphone can bring banking, payments, investing, insurance and financial information into one device.

  • UPI enables instant digital payments.
  • Mobile banking reduces dependence on branches.
  • Cards and contactless payments are widely available.
  • Mutual funds and SIPs can be accessed online.
  • Demat accounts make investing more accessible.
  • Insurance can be researched and purchased online.
  • Financial information is available almost instantly.
Real change: UPI was launched in 2016 and became a major part of India's digital payments infrastructure. 1
→
03
NEXT

Money that works with you

The next stage may be less about opening another app and more about financial services becoming increasingly personalised and automated.

  • AI-assisted financial planning.
  • More personalised financial products.
  • Automated savings and recurring decisions.
  • Voice and conversational financial interfaces.
  • Smarter fraud and risk detection.
  • More integrated payment experiences.
  • Agent-based financial actions may become possible.
Important: These are possibilities based on technological and financial trends, not guaranteed predictions.
