How Did Everyday Life in India Change?

From neighbourhoods, joint families and local markets to smartphones, online shopping and connected lifestyles.

THEN NOW NEXT
EVERYDAY INDIA

Technology changed the lifestyle. People changed with it.

Indian lifestyle has never been one single thing. City, village, income, generation, family structure and culture all influence how people live.

But across generations, some broad changes are easy to notice: how families communicate, shop, eat, work, relax and spend time together.

THE JOURNEY

Everyday life across generations

01
THEN

Life around people and places

Earlier generations often depended heavily on nearby communities, local markets, family networks and face-to-face interaction.

  • Joint families were more common in many communities.
  • Children often played outdoors with neighbourhood friends.
  • Local shops and weekly markets were important.
  • Home cooking dominated everyday meals.
  • Tailors and local craftsmen played important roles.
  • Entertainment was more limited and shared.
  • Communication depended on visits, letters and telephones.
Think about it: Many everyday needs were solved by someone you already knew or by a shop close to home.
→
02
NOW

Life inside the smartphone

Today, a smartphone can connect people to shopping, entertainment, food, work, education and communication.

  • Online shopping complements local shopping.
  • Food delivery is available in many cities.
  • Streaming changed entertainment habits.
  • Social media changed communication and discovery.
  • Remote and hybrid work became more common.
  • Fitness and wellness apps became accessible.
  • Digital services are increasingly part of everyday life.
Current picture: MoSPI's 2025 telecom survey estimated that about 85.5% of Indian households had at least one smartphone and about 86.3% had internet access within the household premises. 2
→
03
NEXT

Life assisted by intelligent technology

The next lifestyle shift may be less about owning more devices and more about technology quietly helping with everyday decisions.

  • AI assistants may coordinate everyday tasks.
  • Homes may become more automated.
  • Shopping may become more personalised.
  • Meal planning may use personal preferences and data.
  • Virtual and augmented experiences may become common.
  • Remote work may continue evolving.
  • Technology may automate more household routines.
But there is another question: Will greater convenience give people more time with each other, or simply give them more things to do?
