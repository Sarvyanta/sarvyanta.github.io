The first moments

Your Baby's First Cry

You may have pictured hearing your baby's first cry. But real birth experiences do not always match the scenes we see in films or hear about from others.

“Your baby's first moments do not have to look like a movie scene to deserve careful, loving attention.”

What does the first cry mean?

Many newborns cry after birth as they adjust to life outside the womb. However, a cry is not the only way healthcare professionals assess a baby's condition. They also observe breathing, heart rate, muscle tone, colour and other signs as appropriate.

What if my baby does not cry immediately?

A baby may need a little time or help with the transition after birth. Sometimes the maternity or newborn team may need to assess or support the baby before you can hold them. The care needed depends on the circumstances.

If your baby does not cry right away, it does not mean you have done anything wrong. The clinical team is there to assess your baby and provide the support needed. Ask them to explain what is happening when circumstances allow.

Why might the first meeting look different?

Some mothers meet their babies immediately. Others may need medical care themselves, or their babies may need assessment or support first. A caesarean birth, an unexpected medical concern or hospital procedures may change when close contact can begin.

If your first moments are different from what you imagined, it is understandable to feel disappointed, worried or overwhelmed. Those feelings deserve kindness and support.

When can I ask to see or hold my baby?

When it is safe and appropriate, ask your maternity team about seeing, touching or holding your baby and about skin-to-skin contact. If direct contact needs to wait, ask what options are possible and how you can stay informed.

Remember the mother, too

After birth, you may feel relief, joy, exhaustion, fear or several emotions together. You do not have to experience one particular feeling to be a loving mother. Give yourself time, and tell a trusted person or healthcare professional if your emotions feel difficult to manage.

Important: Only the trained healthcare team present at birth can assess a newborn's condition. This article is general education and cannot determine whether a particular baby is well. If you are concerned about your baby's breathing, colour, responsiveness or feeding, alert your healthcare team immediately.

Your story is allowed to be different

Whatever happened in those first moments, you deserve to be treated with respect and compassion. You can ask questions later, talk through the experience and seek support if the memories feel upsetting.
