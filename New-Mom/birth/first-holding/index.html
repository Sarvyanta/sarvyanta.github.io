A new connection

Holding Your Baby for the First Time

You may imagine an instant rush of love when you first hold your baby. You may feel that, or you may feel tired, uncertain, overwhelmed or simply quiet. There is no single correct emotional response.

“Bonding is not an exam. You do not have to feel everything at once.”

Let the first moment be your own

Meeting your newborn can be joyful, emotional, surprising or difficult to take in. Birth is physically demanding, and you may still be processing what happened. It is okay if the moment feels quieter or less dramatic than you expected.

Ask for support with the first hold

If you are feeling well and your baby is ready, the maternity team can help you find a comfortable, supported position for holding your baby. Tell them if you feel faint, very tired, in pain or unsure how to support the baby's head and body.

If you have had a caesarean birth or another procedure, ask the team how and when you can safely hold your baby. You may need help adjusting your position or waiting until you are medically ready.

What about skin-to-skin contact?

When appropriate and supported by trained staff, skin-to-skin contact may be one way to begin close contact with your newborn. Ask your care team whether it is suitable at that time and how to keep both you and your baby safe.

If immediate skin-to-skin contact is not possible because you or your baby needs medical attention, that is not a personal failure. Ask the team to explain the situation and discuss when contact may be possible.

What if I do not feel an instant bond?

Some parents feel a strong connection immediately; others feel that the relationship develops gradually through feeding, comforting, learning their baby's cues and spending time together. Fatigue, pain, stress, an unexpected birth experience or emotional difficulties can affect how the first days feel.

You do not need to compare your feelings with another parent's story. If you feel persistently detached, distressed, hopeless or anxious, speak with a healthcare professional. Support is available, and you deserve care as much as your baby does.

Let others care for you, too

  • Accept help with meals, household tasks or practical arrangements.
  • Tell your support person when you need rest or reassurance.
  • Ask questions when something about newborn care feels unfamiliar.
  • Share difficult feelings with someone who will listen without judgement.
Safety note: Follow your maternity team's guidance for holding your newborn and skin-to-skin contact. Do not attempt skin-to-skin contact when you are too sleepy, sedated or unable to hold your baby safely without appropriate help. If you are worried about your or your baby's condition, alert your healthcare team.
