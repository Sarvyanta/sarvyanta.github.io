A reminder for the mother

Your birth story does not have to look like anyone else's

You may have imagined a particular kind of birth. You may have a carefully prepared plan, or you may still be figuring out what feels right for you. Sometimes plans change because of medical needs, and that can bring unexpected feelings.

A vaginal birth, a caesarean birth, a birth that needs extra medical support, or a first meeting that happens later than expected can each be part of a meaningful family story. Your experience does not need to match someone else's to deserve respect and compassionate care.

It is okay to ask questions, request explanations, share concerns and tell your care team what matters to you. When possible, discuss your preferences and support arrangements with your maternity team before labour begins.

Please remember: This website provides general educational information, not individual medical advice. Follow your maternity team's guidance. If you have urgent concerns during pregnancy or labour, contact your maternity unit or local emergency service promptly.

Questions you can ask

  • When should I contact my maternity unit?
  • Who can stay with me during labour?
  • What should I bring to the hospital?
  • How will changes to my birth plan be explained?
  • What support is available after delivery?
