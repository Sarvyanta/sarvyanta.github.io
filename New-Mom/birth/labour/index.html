Preparing for birth

Labour: What to Expect

You may feel excited, nervous, confident, uncertain—or all of these at once. You do not need to predict every moment to prepare for your birth journey.

“You deserve clear information, compassionate care and the freedom to ask questions.”

Every labour experience is different

Labour is the process through which the body prepares for birth and the baby is delivered. Its timing, pace and experience vary. Stories from friends and family may offer reassurance, but they cannot predict exactly how your own birth will unfold.

Learn the broad stages

Healthcare professionals commonly describe labour in stages. The cervix changes as labour progresses, the baby is born, and the placenta is delivered afterward. Your maternity team can explain what is happening and what it means in your particular situation.

Some people experience a gradual beginning, while others need assessment or support sooner. If you are unsure whether labour has started, contact your maternity team for advice rather than relying on a checklist alone.

Make a flexible birth-preference plan

A birth plan can help you communicate your preferences. You might write down who you would like beside you, questions about pain relief, communication preferences, and what matters to you after the baby arrives.

Think of the plan as a conversation starter rather than a promise that every detail will happen exactly as expected. Medical needs can change the plan, and your team should help explain relevant options when possible.

How can a birth partner help?

  • Listen without dismissing your concerns.
  • Help communicate your questions and preferences.
  • Offer reassurance in the way you find helpful.
  • Follow the maternity team's instructions.
  • Help you contact your care team if something worries you.

Questions worth asking before labour

  • When should I call or go to the maternity unit?
  • What pain-relief options are available here?
  • Who can accompany me during labour and birth?
  • What happens if my birth preferences need to change?
  • Who should I contact if I have concerns after discharge?
Important: This article is general education, not individual medical advice. Contact your maternity team promptly for concerns about bleeding, fluid leakage, reduced baby movements, severe pain, or possible labour before your expected time. For heavy bleeding, severe breathing difficulty, seizures, collapse or another emergency, seek emergency medical help.

Be kind to yourself

Preparing for birth is not a test you must pass. Ask for explanations, share what worries you and let trusted people support you. Your well-being matters throughout this experience.
