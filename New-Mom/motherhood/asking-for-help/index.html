YOU DON'T HAVE TO DO IT ALL

Learning to Ask for Help as a Mother

Motherhood can come with an unspoken expectation that you should know what to do and manage everything. But needing support is human, and asking for help can be one way of caring for your family and yourself.

MAKING ROOM FOR SUPPORT

Why asking for help can feel difficult

You may feel that you should be able to manage everything yourself. You may worry about being judged, inconveniencing someone or appearing incapable. Sometimes you may not even know what to ask for because there are so many things to do.

If people around you assume you are managing well, they may not realise how exhausted or overwhelmed you feel. Waiting for others to notice can leave you carrying more than you can comfortably manage.

You are allowed to need help even if you love motherhood, even if other people have helped before, and even if someone else seems to be coping differently.

Asking for support does not mean you are failing. It means you are human.

Make your request specific

People may want to help but may not know what would be useful. A clear request can make it easier for both of you to understand what is needed.

  • “Could you bring us a meal this evening?”
  • “Could you watch the baby while I take a shower and rest?”
  • “Can you pick up these groceries on your way home?”
  • “Could you handle the laundry today? I need a break.”
  • “Can we talk for ten minutes? I need someone to listen.”

You do not have to use these exact words. Choose language that feels natural and appropriate for your relationship.

Accept help without needing to repay it immediately

Support is not always a transaction. If someone you trust offers help, you do not necessarily need to return the favour straight away. You can express appreciation and accept support when you need it.

It is also reasonable to have boundaries. You can appreciate someone's offer while explaining what kind of help would be useful and what would not. Support should not require you to accept behaviour that makes you uncomfortable or unsafe.

Build a small support network

Support can come from different places. A partner, relative, friend, neighbour, parent group or qualified professional may each be able to help in a different way. Not everyone needs to provide every kind of support.

If you do not have dependable help nearby, consider what services or community resources may be available locally. A healthcare professional can also help you explore support if emotional or physical difficulties are making everyday life hard.

When you feel you cannot keep going

If exhaustion or distress becomes persistent or overwhelming, speak with a qualified healthcare professional. If you feel unable to keep yourself or your baby safe, seek urgent assistance and contact a trusted person who can support you immediately.

A gentle reminder: You deserve support before you reach your breaking point. You do not have to prove how much you can carry.
