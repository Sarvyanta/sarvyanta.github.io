Emotional Changes During Pregnancy: Your Feelings Matter

You may be looking forward to meeting your baby while feeling nervous about everything that is changing. You may feel calm one day and emotional the next. You are a person experiencing a major life transition—not a person who must feel happy every minute.

Pregnancy can bring physical changes, new responsibilities, uncertainty and a different picture of the future. For many people, emotions shift during this time. Hormonal changes, sleep, discomfort, previous experiences, relationships and life circumstances can all play a part.

There is no universal emotional experience of pregnancy. Some people feel connected to the pregnancy early on. Others need time. Some feel hopeful and frightened at the same time. Your experience does not need to match someone else's story to be valid.

You can love the life you are preparing for and still find the changes difficult.

Why do my feelings seem to change?

You might find yourself crying more easily, feeling sensitive, worrying about the future or needing more reassurance. These experiences can happen during pregnancy, but they should not be dismissed automatically as “just hormones.” Your feelings deserve attention, especially when they are distressing or affect daily life.

Common feelings you may recognise

Excitement and anticipation

You may imagine meeting your baby, sharing the news or preparing for a new chapter. Enjoy these moments without feeling that you must remain cheerful all the time.

Worry about the unknown

Questions about health, birth, parenting, work or finances can feel large when there are so many unknowns. It may help to separate questions you can address now from things you will learn about later. Your maternity care team can help with pregnancy-related concerns.

Missing your familiar life

You may wonder how your friendships, career, routines or independence will change. Looking forward to motherhood does not mean you must stop valuing the person you were before pregnancy.

Feeling disconnected or unsure

You might not feel the connection you expected, or you might find that your emotions do not match the reactions of people around you. There is no need to force a particular feeling. If disconnection or distress worries you, talking to a supportive professional can help you understand what you are experiencing.

Gentle ways to support yourself

  • Notice your feelings without immediately judging them as good or bad.
  • Talk with someone who listens rather than telling you how you should feel.
  • Take breaks from alarming stories, comparisons and overwhelming online content.
  • Discuss sleep, stress and emotional well-being with your healthcare professional.
  • Ask your partner, family or trusted friends for specific practical help.
  • Make room for activities that help you feel like yourself, when comfortable and appropriate for your pregnancy.

How to explain what you need

It can be difficult to describe a feeling you do not fully understand yourself. You could start with a simple sentence: “I'm not sure exactly what I'm feeling yet, but I need you to listen without trying to fix everything.”

You can also ask for a practical change: “Could you come with me to my appointment?” or “Can we set aside some quiet time to talk about what is worrying me?”

When to reach out for professional help

Contact a healthcare professional if sadness, anxiety, fear or emotional distress is persistent, intense, worsening or interfering with everyday activities. You do not need to wait until things become unbearable to ask for support.

If you feel at risk of harming yourself or someone else, or you cannot keep yourself safe, seek immediate help from local emergency services or a nearby emergency department.

Health information notice: This article is for general education and emotional support. It does not diagnose a mental health condition or replace professional care. A qualified healthcare professional can help assess your individual situation and discuss appropriate support.
