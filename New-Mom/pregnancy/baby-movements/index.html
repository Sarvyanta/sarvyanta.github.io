Baby Movements During Pregnancy: What to Know

The first flutter can feel surprising, joyful or difficult to describe. As pregnancy progresses, noticing your baby's usual movements can become an important part of your awareness of your pregnancy.

Pregnancy is full of experiences that can be hard to explain to someone who has not felt them. Baby movements are one of those experiences. Early movements may feel like gentle flutters, bubbles or small taps. Later, movements may feel more distinct.

Every pregnancy is different. When you first notice movement, how it feels and how it develops can vary. Your maternity care team can explain what is expected for your stage of pregnancy and how to monitor changes appropriately.

You do not have to understand every sensation on your own. Questions are a good reason to reach out to your care team.

When might I first feel movement?

Many pregnant people begin to notice movement around 18–20 weeks, though it can happen earlier or later. In a first pregnancy, movements may not be recognised until later than in a subsequent pregnancy. Placenta position and other individual factors can also affect what you notice.

If you have concerns about not feeling movement when you expected to, contact your maternity care professional for advice specific to your pregnancy.

Getting familiar with your baby's pattern

As pregnancy progresses, you may begin to recognise times when your baby is usually more active. There is no single movement pattern that applies to everyone. Your care team can tell you whether a formal movement-counting method is recommended for your circumstances.

  • Pay attention to the movement pattern that is usual for your baby.
  • Ask your maternity team how they want you to respond if movements change.
  • Keep any movement guidance provided at your antenatal appointments accessible.

When should I seek medical advice?

If your baby's movements are reduced, stop, or change from their usual pattern, contact your maternity unit or healthcare professional immediately for assessment. Do not wait until the next day or rely on an online article to decide whether everything is fine.

Do not rely on a home Doppler or an app to reassure yourself that your baby is well. Hearing a heartbeat at home does not confirm that your baby is healthy.

What if I feel anxious about movement?

It is understandable to want reassurance during pregnancy. If you are worried, contact your maternity care team rather than feeling that you must work it out alone. You are not wasting anyone's time by raising a concern about your baby's movements.

Your questions are worth asking

At your next appointment, you could ask when you should expect to feel movement, how to recognise your baby's usual pattern and exactly whom to contact outside normal clinic hours. Having a clear plan may make it easier to seek help promptly if you need it.

Health information notice: This article provides general educational information, not individual medical advice. Reduced, absent or changed fetal movements require prompt contact with your maternity care team. Follow their instructions and seek urgent assessment when advised.
