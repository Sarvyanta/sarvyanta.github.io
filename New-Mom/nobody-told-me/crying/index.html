When You Cannot Stop Crying

Sometimes the tears come before you can explain what hurts. You can be grateful for your baby and still be having a difficult time.

A gentle reminder for new mothers

You do not always need a clear reason

You may cry while feeding your baby, while trying to fall asleep, while looking at photographs, or when someone asks a simple question about how you are doing.

You might feel exhausted, overwhelmed, lonely, worried, or unable to name what you are feeling. Sometimes everything seems to build up at once.

The transition into motherhood can bring major physical, emotional, and practical changes. Recovery after birth, interrupted sleep, feeding difficulties, and the responsibility of caring for a baby can leave very little room for your own needs.

You are allowed to have complicated feelings

Loving your baby does not mean you will feel happy every moment. Feeling overwhelmed does not mean you are ungrateful. Crying does not mean you are weak.

You do not have to hide your feelings to prove that you are coping. If you can, tell someone you trust that you are having a hard day and explain what would help: company, a meal, help with the baby, or time to rest.

You do not have to be okay every minute to be a loving mother.

Give yourself permission to ask for help

Try to make space for basic needs such as eating, drinking water, resting when possible, and letting another trusted adult take over a task. Small steps cannot solve every difficulty, but you do not need to carry everything alone.

If the crying continues, becomes frequent, or comes with persistent sadness, hopelessness, anxiety, or difficulty managing daily life, speak with your doctor or another qualified healthcare professional. Postpartum depression and anxiety are treatable, and support is available.

When it feels urgent

If you feel you may harm yourself or your baby, or you cannot keep yourself or your baby safe, get immediate help from a trusted adult and local emergency or medical services. Do not stay alone with those feelings.

Remember: You deserve care and understanding, even when you cannot find the words to explain what you feel.
