When Breastfeeding Is Hard

You can love your baby deeply and still find breastfeeding difficult. Both things can be true.

A gentle reminder for new mothers

It may not feel as natural as you expected

Before your baby arrives, breastfeeding can seem like something your body will simply know how to do. Then the baby arrives, and you may find yourself learning through long feeds, uncertainty, tiredness, and questions you never expected to have.

You may worry about whether your baby is getting enough milk, whether the latch is right, or why feeding seems easier for other mothers. These worries can feel especially heavy when you are already recovering from birth and sleeping very little.

Struggling does not mean you are failing

Feeding can take time to work out for both mother and baby. Pain, difficulty latching, concerns about milk supply, and exhaustion are reasons to seek support, not reasons to blame yourself.

A qualified lactation consultant, your doctor, or your baby's paediatrician can help you understand what is happening and discuss appropriate options for you and your baby.

Your wellbeing matters too

You are not required to pretend that feeding is easy. You are allowed to ask someone to sit with you, help with other baby-care tasks, or give you a little time to rest.

Breastfeeding, expressing milk, combination feeding, and formula feeding are different ways families may feed their babies. Your individual situation deserves compassionate, evidence-based advice without shame or pressure.

You are not a bad mother because something that looks simple from the outside feels difficult in real life.

When to get help

Please seek professional advice if feeding is painful, your baby is struggling to feed, or you are worried about weight gain, wet nappies, or milk intake. If your baby is unusually sleepy, difficult to wake, has signs of dehydration, or seems seriously unwell, seek urgent medical care.

A note for you: You do not have to solve every feeding challenge alone. Asking for help is a caring step for both you and your baby.
