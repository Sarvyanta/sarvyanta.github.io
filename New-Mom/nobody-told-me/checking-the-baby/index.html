Checking the Baby Again and Again

You finally have a quiet moment, but instead of resting, you find yourself checking your baby one more time.

A gentle reminder for new mothers

When your mind will not switch off

You look at your sleeping baby. You watch their tiny chest move. You check again a few minutes later, even though you checked just moments ago.

For some mothers, the quiet hours after putting a baby to sleep are not peaceful at all. Their minds fill with questions: Is the baby breathing normally? Are they comfortable? Did I miss something?

Becoming responsible for a tiny, completely dependent person can make you feel alert in ways you have never experienced before. When you are also tired and recovering from birth, worries may feel even louder.

Care and worry can exist together

Wanting to protect your baby is understandable. But if checking becomes constant, prevents you from sleeping, or leaves you feeling frightened even when everything appears okay, you deserve support with those feelings too.

You do not need to feel embarrassed about telling your partner, someone you trust, or a healthcare professional what is happening. Explain how often the worry occurs and how it affects your rest and daily life.

You can be a careful mother without having to carry every fear entirely on your own.

Make room for reassurance and rest

Follow your baby's safe-sleep guidance from your healthcare professional. For babies, this generally includes placing them on their backs for sleep, using a firm, flat sleep surface, and keeping soft bedding and loose objects out of the sleep space.

If another trusted adult is available, talk about sharing baby-care responsibilities so you can get some rest. If you feel driven to check repeatedly even after following safe-sleep guidance, a healthcare professional can help you work through the anxiety.

When to seek support

Speak with a healthcare professional if worries are persistent, difficult to control, or stopping you from sleeping when you have the opportunity. If your baby is struggling to breathe, has blue or grey lips or skin, is unresponsive, or appears seriously unwell, seek emergency medical care immediately.

Remember: Asking for help with anxiety is not a sign that you love your baby any less. You deserve care, too.
