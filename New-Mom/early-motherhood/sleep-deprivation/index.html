REST MATTERS

Sleep Deprivation in Early Motherhood

Broken nights and interrupted naps can make the early months feel exhausting. If you find yourself struggling to concentrate, feeling more emotional or longing for uninterrupted sleep, you are not alone in needing more rest and support.

UNDERSTANDING THE EXHAUSTION

Why broken sleep can feel so difficult

A newborn's feeding and sleeping patterns may be unpredictable. You might fall asleep only to wake again soon, leaving little time to feel rested. Recovery after birth, feeding, household tasks and the mental load of caring for a baby can add to the exhaustion.

Sleep loss can affect mood, patience, memory and concentration. You may find yourself tearful or more easily overwhelmed. This does not mean you are weak or unsuited to motherhood; it means you need rest and support during a demanding period.

Rest is not a reward for finishing everything. It is a real need.

Share the responsibility where possible

If another trusted adult is available, discuss how to share the parts of baby care and household work that can be shared. The best arrangement depends on feeding, health needs, work schedules and your family's circumstances.

  • Agree on who will handle particular tasks during the day or night.
  • Accept help with meals, laundry and errands so rest is more possible.
  • Consider a protected rest period when another trusted adult can care for the baby.
  • Reduce non-essential chores and visitors while you are especially tired.
  • Tell someone clearly when exhaustion is becoming difficult to manage.

Keep sleep and baby safety together

When you are very tired, it can be easy to doze off while feeding or holding your baby. If you feel yourself falling asleep, place your baby in a safe sleep space before you rest. Follow current safe-sleep guidance from your baby's healthcare provider: generally, babies should sleep on their backs on a firm, flat, separate sleep surface without loose bedding or soft objects.

A sofa or armchair is not a safe place to fall asleep with a baby. If you are struggling to stay awake while caring for your baby, ask another trusted adult for help as soon as possible.

Small opportunities for recovery

You may not be able to control when your baby wakes, but you may be able to reduce some demands around those wake-ups. Keep essential supplies nearby, prepare simple meals where practical and allow yourself to rest rather than using every quiet moment for chores.

Try not to compare your sleep or your baby's sleep with another family's experience. Babies differ, and sleep patterns change. If your baby's sleep, feeding or health concerns you, speak with a qualified healthcare professional.

When exhaustion needs more support

If severe tiredness persists, you are unable to function safely, or low mood and anxiety are becoming difficult to manage, contact a healthcare professional. Seek urgent assistance if you feel unable to keep yourself or your baby safe.

A gentle reminder: You do not have to prove that you can manage without rest. Ask for practical help before exhaustion becomes overwhelming.
