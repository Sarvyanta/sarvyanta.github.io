WHEN WORRY FEELS TOO BIG

Anxiety After Birth: Understanding Your Worries

Becoming responsible for a new baby can bring questions and worries. But if your thoughts feel constant, frightening or difficult to switch off, or if anxiety is interfering with daily life, you deserve understanding and appropriate support.

UNDERSTANDING POSTPARTUM ANXIETY

When ordinary worries become overwhelming

It is understandable to think about your baby's feeding, sleep, health and safety. New parents are learning many things at once, and some uncertainty is part of the experience.

Anxiety may need more attention when worry becomes persistent or difficult to manage, when you repeatedly seek reassurance but cannot feel settled, or when fear stops you from resting, leaving home or carrying out everyday activities.

You can take your baby's safety seriously without having to carry every worry alone.

How anxiety can show up

Experiences differ, but anxiety may involve racing thoughts, a sense of dread, irritability, restlessness, difficulty concentrating, physical tension or trouble sleeping even when there is an opportunity to rest.

Some parents experience repeated frightening thoughts about something happening to their baby. Having an unwanted, upsetting thought does not automatically mean you want it to happen. If thoughts are distressing, persistent or difficult to manage, discuss them honestly with a qualified healthcare or mental health professional.

Ways to seek support

  • Tell a trusted person that your worries are becoming difficult to manage.
  • Write down your main concerns to discuss with a healthcare professional.
  • Ask someone to help with practical tasks while you arrange care.
  • Reduce exposure to online content that repeatedly increases your anxiety.
  • Try a simple grounding activity, such as noticing your surroundings and taking slow, comfortable breaths, if it feels helpful.

These ideas may provide some support, but they are not a substitute for professional assessment when anxiety is persistent or severe.

Professional care can help

Speak with a qualified healthcare or mental health professional if anxiety is affecting your sleep, relationships, ability to care for yourself or daily functioning. They can help assess what is happening and discuss suitable options, which may include therapy, medical treatment or other forms of support based on your needs.

If you experience severe confusion, hallucinations, unusual beliefs or feel unable to keep yourself or your baby safe, seek urgent medical care. If you have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department.

You do not need to wait until you are at breaking point

You can ask for help even if other people think you are coping well. You can also seek support if you cannot clearly explain why you feel anxious. Your experience deserves attention without judgement.

A gentle reminder: Asking for help with anxiety is a way to care for yourself and your family. You do not have to handle every worry alone.
